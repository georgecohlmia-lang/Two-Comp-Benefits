01The role you're designing comp for

This worksheet designs the full comp picture for one role. Name it so the design is concrete.

02Base bands — floor, midpoint, ceiling

Three numbers and a rationale. Set the bands honestly from market data and your network — without bands, every salary becomes a negotiation, and the loudest negotiators get paid the most.

Band width (ceiling − floor as % of midpoint) —
Typical bands at owner-led scale run 30–50% wide. Narrower than that means real outliers fall outside; wider than that means the bands stop disciplining anything.
03Variable comp — designed to actually move behavior

Pay on outcomes the person can actually influence. Math simple enough they can calculate it themselves. The smaller-business edge is the proximity between effort and outcome — use it.

04Equity substitute — a real stake without giving up the company

For most acquired small businesses, the simplest first move is the cash profit-share. Sometimes it's the only piece needed. Choose the vehicle, then make it specific enough that the person can describe it to their spouse.

05Benefits philosophy — where you choose to compete

You can't match the comprehensive packages a holding firm offers. Don't try. Spend disproportionately where you genuinely win — and be honest about what you're choosing not to do.

06Annual review cadence — install it before pressure forces it

Reviews that happen when someone complains or a counter-offer arrives send the wrong signal: pay moves through pressure, not performance. Set the window and tell the team it will happen on that schedule.
